# L.3.5a — Real and Imaginary Meanings  (grade 3rd Grade)

## [intro] heading: "Literal vs Nonliteral"
  AUDIO: Some phrases mean exactly what they say. Others paint a picture in your mind!
    SCREEN: Two kinds of meaning
  AUDIO: Literal means the exact, real meaning of the words.
    SCREEN: Literal: exact meaning
  AUDIO: Nonliteral means the words paint a picture and mean something different.
    SCREEN: Nonliteral: a word picture

## [teach] heading: "Words That Paint"
  AUDIO: Listen to this phrase. The classroom was a zoo this morning.
    SCREEN: The room was a zoo
  AUDIO: Were there really lions and monkeys in the room? No! So it is not literal.
    SCREEN: Real animals? No!
  AUDIO: It is nonliteral. It really means the room was wild and noisy.
    SCREEN: Means: wild and noisy

## [example] heading: "Let's Try One"
  AUDIO: Let's try one together. Here's a phrase.
  AUDIO: It was raining cats and dogs all afternoon.
    SCREEN: Raining cats and dogs
  AUDIO: Are cats and dogs really falling from the sky? No! So this is nonliteral.
    SCREEN: Literal or not? / Nonliteral!
  AUDIO: What does it really mean? It is raining very hard!
    SCREEN: Real meaning? / Raining very hard!
  AUDIO: The words sound silly literally, so the meaning is nonliteral. You pictured it!

## [tip] heading: "Spot the Picture"
  AUDIO: Here is a helpful trick. If the words sound silly when you picture them literally, the meaning is nonliteral!
    SCREEN: Sounds silly? Nonliteral!
