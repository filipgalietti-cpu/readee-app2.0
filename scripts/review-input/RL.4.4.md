# RL.4.4 — Word Meanings in Stories  (grade 4th Grade)

## [intro] heading: "Meaning Beyond the Word"
  AUDIO: Authors use words for more than just their basic meaning.
    SCREEN: More than basic meaning
  AUDIO: Authors use words in fun and surprising ways to add deeper meaning to their stories.
    SCREEN: Words add deeper meaning

## [teach] heading: "Allusions"
  AUDIO: An allusion is a quick reference to a story or myth everyone knows.
    SCREEN: Allusion: quick reference
  AUDIO: Calling someone a Hercules means they are super strong!
    SCREEN: Hercules = super strong

## [teach] heading: "Figurative Meanings"
  AUDIO: Words like soared, drowning, or melting often mean something figurative.
    SCREEN: Words mean something figurative
  AUDIO: Figurative language uses words in a way that is not literal.
    SCREEN: Figurative language is not literal
  AUDIO: It helps create a special effect or meaning in a story.
    SCREEN: Creates special effect or meaning

## [example] heading: "Let's Try One"
  AUDIO: Let's try one together. Here is a sentence from a story.
  AUDIO: Maria was drowning in homework all weekend.
    SCREEN: Maria was drowning in homework all weekend.
  AUDIO: Is there real water here? No. This is not literal. There is no actual water.
    SCREEN: Real water? / No, not literal.
  AUDIO: So what does it mean? She had way too much homework, like it was burying her.
    SCREEN: What does it mean? / Too much homework!
  AUDIO: Drowning paints a feeling, not real water. You unlocked the figurative meaning!

  AUDIO: Here is a helpful trick.
  AUDIO: If a word seems too big or strange, look it up. Or use context to guess.
