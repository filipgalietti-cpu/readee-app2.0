# L.3.2 — Quotes and Commas  (grade 3rd Grade)

## [intro] heading: "Marks That Speak"
  AUDIO: When a character talks in a story, two little marks show us their exact words.
    SCREEN: Marks that show talking
  AUDIO: Quotation marks are the curly marks that wrap around the spoken words.
    SCREEN: Quotation marks wrap words
  AUDIO: A comma is a tiny pause mark that helps set off who is speaking.
    SCREEN: Comma = pause mark

## [teach] heading: "Wrap the Words"
  AUDIO: Put quotation marks around the exact words a person says, and nothing else.
    SCREEN: Quotes around spoken words
  AUDIO: Maya said, open quote, Hi, close quote. The quotation marks hug only the word Hi.
    SCREEN: Maya said, "Hi."

## [teach] heading: "Comma Before the Quote"
  AUDIO: When the speaker comes first, put a comma right after the speaking word, before the quotation marks open.
    SCREEN: Speaker first, then comma
  AUDIO: Dad said, comma, open quote, Run, close quote. The comma goes after said, before the quote.
    SCREEN: Dad said, "Run!"

## [example] heading: "Let's Try One"
  AUDIO: Let's try one together. Here's a sentence missing its marks.
    SCREEN: Add the marks
  AUDIO: Tom said Stop.
    SCREEN: Tom said Stop
  AUDIO: Who speaks first? Tom! So a comma goes right after said.
    SCREEN: Comma after? / said,
  AUDIO: What word does Tom say? Stop! So quotation marks wrap around Stop.
    SCREEN: Wrap what? / "Stop!"
  AUDIO: Now it reads: Tom said, comma, open quote, Stop, close quote. You fixed it!
    SCREEN: Tom said, "Stop!"

## [tip] heading: "Say It, Then Mark It"
  AUDIO: Here's a trick. Read it out loud and pause where the speaker stops talking. That pause is your comma.
    SCREEN: Pause = comma spot
  AUDIO: Then hug just the spoken words with quotation marks. Now you're ready!
    SCREEN: Hug the spoken words
